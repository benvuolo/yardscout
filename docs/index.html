About the numbers

Every dollar figure is what the same used part goes for online — eBay sold listings, nationwide, working condition — i.e. what you’d pay if you didn’t pull it yourself. Local prices vary, so check your own area too. Condition decides everything: a cracked, faded, or dead part is scrap, and none of these figures are a guarantee. Pull prices are shown only when they come from that yard’s own published price list; otherwise you’ll see “check yard price list” and the figure is the online price alone (no pull price subtracted, so what you actually save depends on the gate price).

About coverage

YardScout tracks every major self-service chain with online inventory — Pick-n-Pull, LKQ Pick Your Part, and Pull-A-Part (including former U-Pull-&-Pay yards) — plus regional chains and a growing set of independent yards: 210+ yards nationwide. The full list is at yardscout.io/yards, with a form to ask for a yard we’re missing. What landed this week, by make: yardscout.io/landed.

About freshness

Inventory is re-scanned every 4 hours — the “Updated” stat above shows the last scan time. Parts listed on a car are what it originally came with; yards track cars, not remaining parts, so a part may already be pulled by the time you visit. Yard pull prices come from each chain’s own published price sheet, refreshed with the scans. Online prices are curated estimates of what the same used part goes for (typical eBay sold prices) — not live quotes.

Always inspect a part on the car before you buy. Inventory reflects the last scan and yards sell or crush cars daily.
