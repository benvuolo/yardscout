Privacy policy

Last updated November 13, 2026

YardScout is built so we know as little about you as possible. This page says exactly what stays on your phone, what leaves it, and why. No legalese tricks.

Your location

When you enter a zip code or use the location button, the distance to each yard is computed in your browser, on your device. Browsing never sends your zip code or GPS position to us. The only network request a zip code triggers is a lookup of that zip's public latitude/longitude from zippopotam.us (a free public zip-code database), which learns nothing but the zip itself.

There are two exceptions, and each happens only if you ask for it: push alerts in the native app, and the free Monday email on the website (both below). A watch is defined by a centre point and a radius, and to send you alerts or the email our server has to know that centre. It is sent rounded to roughly 1 km — never your exact GPS fix — and only once you turn alerts on or confirm the email.

Your saved cars and watchlist

Hearted cars, your vehicle watchlist, and your settings live in your browser's local storage on your device; clearing your browser data (or deleting the app) deletes them. On the web they never leave your device, with one exception you opt into: if you ask for the free Monday email after setting a watch, a copy of that watch's criteria goes to our server with your email address (see “The free Monday email” below). In the native app, turning on push alerts mirrors your watch criteria (make, model, year range, the rounded centre and radius) to our server so alerts can be matched — turning alerts off removes them.

No trackers, no ads, no data sales

We don't run third-party analytics or trackers, we don't run ads, we don't sell data, and we don't share data with data brokers.

To see whether the app is working for people, we keep our own anonymous daily tallies of a few actions: a first visit, a zip entered, a part search, a watch created, the Monday email offered, asked for or confirmed, the Pro screen opened, a checkout or app-store link tapped, (since November 13, 2026) how the Cars list was narrowed: by the search box, the filters, a car link or the trim filter (each counted at most once a day per device), and (from November 13, 2026) how a checkout ended: bought, cancelled, waiting for approval, not completed, payment not yet confirmed, or failed (with only a general reason such as “network” or “store error”, never the error itself), and in the apps whether the store's price failed to load. Each tally is a count per day, with the platform (web, iPhone or Android) and, if you arrived through a tagged link, that link's source name. No account, device identifier, IP address, zip code or search term is stored with them, so a tally can't be tied to you or your device.

What the app fetches

The app downloads the yard inventory data file and yard price lists from our static hosting (GitHub Pages), and fonts from Google Fonts. Those requests carry the standard technical information any web request does (IP address, browser type), governed by GitHub's privacy policy and Google Fonts' privacy notes. We don't see or store any of it.

Vehicle photos are loaded directly from each yard's own website or image server (for example LKQ Pick Your Part or Pick-n-Pull's image host) when you view a car that has one. Like any image on the web, that request sends your IP address and browser type to the yard's server, under that yard's own privacy policy. We don't proxy, copy or track these requests.

Push alerts and the native apps (iOS and Android)

The YardScout apps work without an account — no name, email, or phone number, ever. If you enable push alerts in the app, we store three things on our server: an anonymous device identifier (a random ID generated on your device that we cannot link to you), the push notification token Apple (APNs) or Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging) issues for delivering your alerts, and your watch criteria (the vehicles, the rounded centre point and the radius you asked to be alerted about). That's the complete list, and it's used only to send the alerts you requested. Turning alerts off, or deleting the app, invalidates the token and the record is pruned automatically; you can also email us to have it deleted immediately.

Web push on the website uses your browser's push service in the same way (an anonymous subscription plus your watch criteria) and only if you turn it on.

The free Monday email (website)

After you set a watch on the website you can leave your email address for a free email every Monday listing the cars that match that watch and were set out at yards near you. If you do, we store exactly this: your email address, a copy of that watch's criteria (make, model, year range, the zip centre rounded to roughly 1 km and the radius, as for alerts above), the dates you signed up and confirmed, if you arrived through a tagged link that link's source name until you confirm (it is then deleted; since November 13, 2026, so sign-ups can be counted per link), and a list of the cars we have already emailed you about so none is sent twice. We use them only to send that email. We send one confirmation email first and nothing else until you click it; an address that is never confirmed is deleted after 7 days. Every Monday email has a one-click unsubscribe link: it deletes your address and everything stored with it. No account, password or IP address is stored, and the watch saved in your browser is not changed. The emails go out through our email provider, Resend.

Pro subscriptions

On iOS, subscriptions are processed entirely by Apple through the App Store; on Android, by Google through Google Play. We never see your payment details — the stores send us only a receipt that proves the subscription is active. On the web, payments are handled by Stripe, which sends us your email address so we can confirm Pro on your devices; we don't receive card numbers.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Material changes will be called out in the app.
