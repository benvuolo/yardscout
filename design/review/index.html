Three redesign directions, side by side with today’s app

Same logo, same palette. New type scale, spacing, components and layout. Every screen uses real cars from the Nov 6 scan around zip 95112.

Design exploration · 2026-11-06 · branch design/redesign-2026-11 · nothing shipped

Recommendation

Build A, Pull Sheet. Then add B’s “walk by row” yard view as a screen inside it. Keep C’s garage idea for later.

A fixes the biggest problems: you can’t find the row number, cards have no clear main button, and the first screen is a wall of text. It also keeps today’s four tabs, so it can ship in small CSS-first steps. B needs a real map library and tiles. C needs a new “garage” record and changes to how alerts are set up. Both are bigger bets.

Audit of today’s app

I tested on an iPhone 13 viewport and at 1280px: first load, search by zip, results, a car card, Parts, Alerts, Yards, the Pro sheet and Saved. Here is what makes it look “vibecoded”:

Reads as generated

  • Too many badge styles: NEW, Row box, “Gets picked over fast” pill, lock link, demand rail, mono stat tiles
  • A coloured left rail on every card, plus a dashed hazard stripe under the header
  • Monospace used for whole sentences (“Set out Nov 5 · Day 2 of ~100 typical”, loading text, the yard tools line)
  • Five button styles: black, grey, orange-outline, orange-tint and underlined links
  • Big stat tiles whose numbers change meaning between tabs, and sometimes all read 0
  • Font sizes from 11 to 28px with no scale, and uneven card padding

Keep these

  • The logo, the palette, and the existing dark theme
  • Honest copy: “a guide, never a promise”, and “if equipped”
  • The lifespan line (“Day 2 of ~100 typical”). No one else has it. It deserves a visual.
  • Four bottom tabs, VIN copy, links to the yard’s own listing
  • Accurate set-out dates and the 4-hour scan cadence

Top 10 problems, ranked by impact on users

  1. The first screen is four messages before a single carA coverage paragraph, the zip box, a “How to read this” notice, and an App Store banner that covers the lower half. There is also a Pro link. A new user has to read about 120 words before seeing a car.
  2. The free car card hides its main value behind blur“9 parts to check” opens to a disclaimer and rows of “•••• ••••”. To a new user this looks broken, not premium.
  3. No clear main action on a carAbout eight things to tap: bell, share, heart, the Pro link, the parts toggle, the yard link, VIN copy. The three icon buttons are about 24px, below the 44pt minimum. Heart (save for the visit) and bell (watch the model) are easy to mix up.
  4. The row number is hard to findIn the yard, the row is what you need. Today it is a small box that wraps after the distance (“13 / mi Row 125”).
  5. The radius and counts don’t agreeThe copy says 250 miles and the filter says 100. Stats show 17,778 “near you”, and the header says “17 yards in your ran…”, cut off. Results start with Newark (13 mi) even though San Jose North is 1 mi away. The sort isn’t shown.
  6. The header subtitle changes meaning on every tab“334,432 cars on row”, then “22,544 new this week”, then “scanned 2h ago”. It’s in uppercase mono and often cut off.
  7. Alerts start with setup, not with what you’ll getFirst a paragraph and a browser-notification button. Then a long stack of select boxes. Then two dashed empty states and three tiles that all read 0.
  8. Part finder is a form split from car searchSeven fields with dropdowns, and you have to pick an exact suggestion (“Pick a part first”). People think “alternator for my Civic”, but Parts and Cars are separate tabs.
  9. The Pro sheet buries the price and “Already have Pro?”There are about 70 words before the plan and seven dense bullets. Restore comes last, after the App Store block.
  10. Hard to use outdoorsLight grey 12px mono text, borders as thin as hairlines, and small targets. Field-app guidance says glare washes out low contrast and thin type. Gloves need targets larger than 44pt.

What changes for users

In the yard

  • Row number is the largest thing on each car, so you can read it at arm’s length
  • Parts become a checklist you tick as you pull. “If equipped” parts sit in their own dashed group and add $0, as today.
  • One big orange button (A: “Add to pull sheet”, B: “Directions to row 17”) at the bottom, in thumb reach
  • Every target is 44–52px. No body text below 14px. Mono only for numbers.

Planning at home

  • One search box for “part or car”. Results answer the question directly: “6 donor cars for your alternator”.
  • The days-on-lot line becomes a small meter: fresh, mid-stay, leaving soon
  • Watches show what is on lots now, not just the criteria
  • The Pro sheet leads with the price and four benefits. Restore is one tap.

Effort and risk

app.js is about 490 KB of hand-written JS that renders through template strings, with styles.css at 85 KB. Every direction can start with the same token layer (design/shared/base.css).

Suggested path for A, in small releases

  1. 1. Tokens onlyAdd the type, spacing, radius and touch-target tokens to styles.css :root. No visual change. Every later step uses them.
  2. 2. Base stylesOne button system, Inter for all sentences and mono for numbers only. Remove the card rails and hazard stripe. Raise targets to 44px or more. Changes are CSS only, with the same DOM.
  3. 3. Car cardRewrite the card template: row plate, lifespan meter, one primary action, overflow menu for share. Bump the sw.js cache name.
  4. 4. Car detail and parts checklistPushed screen with a sticky action bar. Keep the “if equipped” group as it is.
  5. 5. First run and searchZip as a chip in the header. Combined part-or-car search. Remove the notice stack.
  6. 6. Watches, Pro sheet, yard viewHits shown on watch cards. Shorter Pro sheet. Optionally B’s walk-by-row list for a single yard.

Before each step, compare Playwright screenshots of today’s screens with the new ones. The scripts used for this review are on the branch.

Questions for you

  1. Free tier: today free users see blurred part names. Should free users see part names and have only prices and demand locked? That one change would remove most of the “broken” feeling on the car card.
  2. Pull sheet: should A rename “Saved” to “Pull sheet” and group it by yard? Data and storage stay the same.
  3. Are you happy to leave the map (B) for a later release? A yard’s own row layout isn’t in our data, so B’s “walk by row” can only sort by row number.

How the directions were grounded

Users are DIY mechanics, flippers and project-car people. They plan at home, then use the app one-handed in sun glare at the yard, sometimes with gloves or greasy hands and weak signal. Principles taken from field-app and saved-search practice, not any app’s visuals:

  • Primary actions and navigation sit in the bottom thumb zone. Strong borders on controls, so they survive glare. Text at 16px or larger, and no thin grey text (Glance, Sympli, Apario).
  • Saved searches show what matches now, allow per-search alert controls, and avoid alert fatigue (Sweetwater Gear Exchange, Perchwell).
